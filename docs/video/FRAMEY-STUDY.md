# Framey reference: deeper motion study

20 November 2026. Source: https://x.com/liu8in/status/2123562606535565629/video/1?s=46

This is an analysis of the supplied video, not a verified account of how Framey generated it. A finished reel cannot prove the editor, model, prompting, labor, cost or automation behind it. Reference footage remains study-only and is not bundled in Skye or the new proof.

## The important difference

The reference is sentence-directed, not merely beat-cut. Large nouns arrive near spoken stresses, small linking words form a quiet second hierarchy, and whole phrases accumulate before the next emphasis replaces them. Footage changes with the sentence's idea. Subject scale, background and text geometry change together. More random movement alone would not reproduce that direction.

## Method and timing limits

The file is 1920×1080, 25 fps, approximately 41.37 seconds. I inspected a 2 fps timestamped overview, the earlier 4 fps overview, and 12/30 fps strips near the mosaic, first picture change and upside-down section. FFmpeg scene-score candidates were measured on source frames. The table distinguishes measured picture-change frames from approximate word windows. Word timings below are visual windows, generally about ±0.25 seconds, not a frame-perfect transcription of every glyph. Motion curves are visual inferences, not recovered project values. The narration was transcribed. Audio rhythm conclusions are limited to the narration and timing, not a listening-reviewed music mix or a proven BPM.

## Reusable mechanics and what they need

| Priority | Mechanic | Needs | Skye status after this work |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phrase hierarchy + staggered entrance/exit | Phrase groups, independent start/end times, positions, scale/color/angle | Built-in Compose generates separate editable text page clips with native boxes, overrides and CSS keyframes. No user-written scene code. |
| 1 | Directional snap in / soft overshoot / accelerated sweep out | Fast deceleration on arrival, short hold, accelerating exit, blur/opacity | Built into Compose. Curves are our own approximation, not recovered reference curves. |
| 1 | Sentence-level composition changes | Copy split into phrase groups and scene durations | Eight layout choices in structured recipes. Human/agent still decides meaning and footage choice. |
| 1 | Native footage push, split, cards and tile wall | Project media paths, duration, source range, layout | Separate native footage clips with native animation. No canvas-embedded videos. |
| 1 | Readable text over a picture flip | Independent footage and text layers | Native footage rotate keyframes, unaffected text layers. |
| 2 | Short negative interruption | Defined flash timing and invert state | Native footage filter animation, including cards/tiles. Needs contrast/strobe review per delivery. |
| 2 | Block interruption | Tile geometry, color, deterministic reveal order | Built-in short overlay page clip. It is a graphic cover/reveal, not optical-flow or exact scene-to-scene block correspondence. |
| 2 | Beat grid | BPM and beats per scene, or explicit durations | Built-in arithmetic grid. Manual BPM, not automatic music analysis or speech alignment. |
| 2 | Curved phrase arrangement | Word positions/rotations around a subject | Arc layout arranges phrase groups. True per-letter path typography remains in Cut Rush's code template, not yet a native editable whole-line curve control. |
| 3 | Text behind actor / isolated flying phone | Per-frame subject/object alpha, tracking, depth order | Existing prepared-matte tools can be used separately. Compose does not create a clean subject/object matte. No new automatic occlusion claim. |
| 3 | Speech-directed type and cut selection | Word timestamps, emphasis, semantic grouping, editorial choice | Still missing. Current proof is an original instrumental style exercise, not lip/speech-synced direction. |
| 3 | Contextual shot generation / acting / art direction | Suitable source footage, rights, possibly generation | Still supplied by human/agent. Two stock clips cannot reproduce the reference's actor performance and scene variety. |

## Color, scale and rhythm

The palette is organized by scene, not randomized: cream/red opening, textured deep red phone, warm desk, yellow/black then mint/black silhouette, warm rug, cool blue room, natural city/sky, warm calendar, teal window, warm product desk, neutral talking head, clouds. Mint is the recurring emphasis anchor; white usually carries connectors. Red is a contrast event rather than a universal accent.

The large condensed words often occupy roughly half the picture height, while connectors can be under a tenth. Those are visual estimates, not extracted font sizes or a claim about the exact typeface. The important reusable control is the size ratio and safe placement. The reference sometimes crops background type deliberately; foreground message cropping is a different problem and should be rejected.

Arrivals appear fast with deceleration, often around a few tenths of a second. Exits can enlarge/blur and overlap a new focus, giving depth. Some words persist for a whole clause. The final CTA is allowed to settle. The measured picture intervals are irregular, about 0.76–4.48 seconds between major candidates, and that is why applying the same two-second cut to everything misses the pacing. The narration is the clearest timing scaffold. No reliable music BPM was established in this study.

## Proof boundary

The third proof is built from a blank project with imported licensed stock, the new Compose form, a native audio bed, and native export. It demonstrates reusable construction without handwriting scene HTML/JS. It is not a claim that the default seven-scene preset is better art direction than the hand-designed Cut Rush. It trades some custom direction for editability and repeatability. The text remains the editor's native HTML/text-page clip model, not a new primitive text codec. Each phrase has a separate timeline clip and editable DOM text override.
